About this lecture

This lecture is on simple linear regression, taught on October 6, 2025 to full-time MBA students for their GSBA 524 class. I use a document camera in the MBA classroom rather than the whiteboard, since students seated in the back have previously reported difficulty seeing the board, particularly from certain angles. I teach three consecutive sections from 8 AM to 12:30 PM and reuse the same handwritten notes, so the video includes pre-existing handwritten annotations.

In simple linear regression, the goal is to predict an attribute of interest based on one explanatory variable. In this lecture, we use a dataset to predict the prices of small diamonds (similar cut and clarity) based on their weight in carats. We begin by reviewing the equation of a line and clarifying the meanings of slope and intercept, then derive the least-squares estimators from a sample and discuss why they are not robust.

Next, students complete a hands-on exercise on their laptops. We load the data in R, use GitHub Copilot to estimate the regression line and generate a summary of the regression output, and then interpret each component of that output together. A particular focus is explaining the uncertainty, or variability, associated with regression coefficient estimates. We build on this discussion to introduce the t-test for evaluating the statistical significance of the explanatory variable.

The class blends hands-on, software-based implementation with statistical theory about uncertainty when working with samples. Simple linear regression is a crucial topic: it is the foundational statistical model on which the broader range of machine-learning models is built. It is especially important that students leave this lecture with a clear appreciation of the uncertainty and variability inherent in statistical estimates. Building on this lecture, the next class covers multiple linear regression, which involves several explanatory variables.
